Arm to corner

For each pair of capitals, the app looks for up to three small changes that remove knives (material thinner than 0.8 mm: slivers and sharp wedges) and cuts (air slots thinner than 0.8 mm) without distorting the letters much. A change moves a flat edge of either letter onto a feature of the other (a flat edge, or a pointed corner like the notch between a B's bowls), shifts a whole stroke to do that, or has both letters meet halfway. It prefers lining edges up (the thin part vanishes) over clearing them by 0.8 mm (the thin part thickens), moving the odd letter out rather than one that sits on a line the font's capitals share, and keeping strokes their thickness. Tops and bottoms never move; same-letter pairs are left alone. Changed letters are blue (dashed: as drawn); lines mark the new heights. The % is the largest change in any band's height. The 3D views show the pair's solid from the front-right; click one to orbit it. Under each card, knives and cuts before → after in mm³ (green: better, red: worse).
